\begin{abstract}
Landslides are among the most dangerous natural hazards, damaging infrastructure, environment, and human life. This paper presents an FPGA-based data acquisition and DSP system for landslide detection using the magnetotelluric (MT) method, processing natural EM field variations in real time. The architecture (Xilinx Artix-7) includes EM sensors, signal conditioning, a 10-bit 100\,MSPS ADC, buffering, 2048-point FFT computation, magnitude estimation, and UART communication to detect abnormal EM variations linked to landslide activity. Verified through simulation and hardware implementation in Vivado, the design shows reliable high-speed operation, efficient resource utilisation, and low power-making it a strong, scalable platform for future landslide early-warning applications.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
FPGA, Landslide Detection, Magnetotelluric (MT), Data Acquisition System, Digital Signal Processing (DSP), Fast Fourier Transform (FFT), Real-Time Monitoring, Artix-7 FPGA.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}

Landslides cause significant loss of life and infrastructure damage, particularly in tectonically active regions such as the Himalayan foothills. Reliable early warning requires continuous, real-time monitoring of subsurface changes preceding slope failure. The Magnetotelluric (MT) method is a passive electromagnetic technique that characterises subsurface resistivity without artificial source infrastructure~\cite{Tikhonov1950,Cagniard1953}, making it well suited for landslide monitoring.

MT records five field components ($E_x$, $E_y$, $H_x$, $H_y$, $H_z$) over $10^{-3}$\,Hz to 10\,kHz, with depth sensitivity $\delta \approx 503\sqrt{\rho/f}$ (m), enabling investigation of both shallow slip surfaces and deeper structures. Li et al.~\cite{LiuCheng2024} presented an EM acquisition circuit for landslide monitoring but lacked a complete FPGA-based real-time DSP architecture. Liu et al.~\cite{ChenWang2020} proposed an FPGA-based multi-channel system with wireless telemetry, limited to low bandwidth and sampling rates. Ren et al.~\cite{RenZhang2020} introduced a lightweight Zynq SoC-based acquisition module lacking an integrated high-speed MT processing pipeline. A need therefore remains for a low-power, high-speed FPGA platform integrating real-time MT acquisition with on-chip DSP for continuous landslide monitoring.




This paper presents an FPGA-based MT acquisition and DSP system for landslide detection on a Xilinx Artix-7 (Digilent Nexys\,4\,DDR), offering low-power, high-speed, real-time on-chip processing suited for portable, long-term monitoring. The system performs entirely on-chip:
\begin{itemize}
    \item 10-bit ADC capture at 100\,MSPS (single-channel);
    \item 2048-point pipelined radix-2 FFT for real-time spectral analysis;
    \item CORDIC-based magnitude computation
          $|X[k]| = \sqrt{\mathrm{Re}^2[k] + \mathrm{Im}^2[k]}$;
    \item Trigger-based acquisition with adjustable gain and threshold;
    \item UART interface for host-PC transfer and configuration;
    \item Estimated on-chip power of 0.333\,W (Vivado post-implementation netlist analysis), enabling energy-efficient continuous monitoring.
\end{itemize}


Section II presents the theoretical background of the Magnetotelluric (MT) method. Section III details the proposed FPGA architecture and its sub-modules. Section IV presents the simulation, hardware implementation, and post-implementation results for on-chip FPGA. Section V concludes the paper and outlines directions for future work. Finally, section VI provides the acknowledgment of the financial support received for this research.

\section{Magnetotelluric Method: Theoretical Background}
The MT method is a passive EM technique using natural electric and magnetic fields to infer subsurface resistivity~\cite{Tikhonov1950, Cagniard1953}. Fields above 1\,Hz arise from thunderstorm activity, below 1\,Hz from solar wind–magnetosphere interaction~\cite{Simpson2005, Vozoff1972}. The field decays exponentially with depth via the skin depth~\cite{Hohmann1988}:
\begin{equation}
    \delta = 503\,\sqrt{\frac{\rho}{f}} \quad [\text{m}],
\end{equation}
\noindent where $\rho$ ($\Omega\cdot$m) is resistivity and $f$ (Hz) is frequency, giving depth sensitivity from metres to tens of kilometres~\cite{Simpson2005, Vozoff1972}. The impedance tensor $\mathbf{Z}(\omega)$ relates orthogonal field components~\cite{Tikhonov1950, Cagniard1953}:
\begin{equation}
    \begin{pmatrix} E_x \\ E_y \end{pmatrix}
    =
    \begin{pmatrix} Z_{xx} & Z_{xy} \\
                    Z_{yx} & Z_{yy} \end{pmatrix}
    \begin{pmatrix} H_x \\ H_y \end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
\vspace{-7mm}
\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.40\textwidth]{mtSetup.png}
  \caption{Schematic of a five-component MT field deployment showing
           orthogonal electric dipoles ($E_x$, $E_y$), three-axis
           induction coil magnetometers ($B_x$, $B_y$, $B_z$), and
           a central data logger at a single survey site~\cite{Simpson2005}.}
  \label{fig:MT_field_layout}
\end{figure}
\vspace{-4mm}
Apparent resistivity and phase are:
\begin{align}
    \rho_{a,ij} &= \frac{1}{\mu_0\,\omega}\bigl|Z_{ij}\bigr|^2, \\
    \phi_{ij}   &= \arctan\!\left(
    \frac{\mathrm{Im}[Z_{ij}]}{\mathrm{Re}[Z_{ij}]}\right),
\end{align}

\section{Proposed FPGA-Based Acquisition Architecture}

\subsection{System Overview}

The proposed system is implemented on a \textbf{Xilinx Artix-7} FPGA (Digilent Nexys\,4\,DDR) for on-chip MT-based landslide monitoring~\cite{LiuCheng2024}: a 10-bit ADC input at 100\,MSPS feeds a 2048-point real-time FFT, and spectral magnitude is transmitted to a host PC via UART~\cite{ChenWang2020}.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.90\columnwidth]{I2.png}
    \caption{Top-level block diagram of the proposed FPGA-based data acquisition and DSP system.}
    \label{fig:fpga_block}
\end{figure}

As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:fpga_block}, the ADC input is sampled, buffered, and processed by the FFT core, with resulting magnitudes sent via UART for real-time display. The signal chain is:
\vspace{1mm}
\begin{equation}
\underbrace{\text{ADC}}_{\scriptscriptstyle 100\text{M,}10b}
\!\xrightarrow{\scriptscriptstyle\texttt{rd\_adc}}\!
\underbrace{\text{RAM}}_{\scriptscriptstyle 2048}
\!\xrightarrow{\scriptscriptstyle\texttt{dec}}\!
\underbrace{\text{FFT}}_{\scriptscriptstyle 2048\text{pt}}
\!\xrightarrow{\scriptscriptstyle\text{Re,Im}}\!
\underbrace{|X(k)|}_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{Sq,Sum,Rt}}
\!\xrightarrow{\scriptscriptstyle\texttt{UART}}\!
\text{PC}
\label{eq:signal_chain}
\end{equation}


\subsection{Sub-Module Descriptions}

The design is partitioned into nine sub-modules: \texttt{PLL\_loop} generates 50/100/200\,MHz clocks; \texttt{read\_adc} captures 10-bit ADC samples; \texttt{RAM\_ADC} buffers 2048 samples; \texttt{decoder} converts binary-offset to two's complement; \texttt{FFT} performs a 2048-point pipelined radix-2 transform; \texttt{Square/Sum} and \texttt{Root} compute the magnitude, stored in \texttt{RAM\_FFT}; \texttt{serial\_tx/rx} handles UART communication; and \texttt{TOP} provides FSM control and integration.
\vspace{-2mm}
\subsection{Finite State Machine Control}
\vspace{-1mm}
Three co-operating FSMs in the TOP module govern the workflow. \textbf{FSM\,1}, on command \texttt{`P'}, sequences: \textit{Wait} $\rightarrow$ \textit{Trig} $\rightarrow$ \textit{Acq} $\rightarrow$ \textit{FFT} $\rightarrow$ \textit{FFT\_write} $\rightarrow$ \textit{Square} $\rightarrow$ \textit{Send} $\rightarrow$ \textit{Wait}; commands \texttt{A}--\texttt{D} set time-base, trigger level, slope, and gain in \textit{Wait}. \textbf{FSM\,2}, on the 100\,MHz ADC clock, fills \texttt{RAM\_ADC} with 2048 samples during \textit{Acq}, else computes $\Delta x = x(k+1)-x(k)$ for trigger detection. \textbf{FSM\,3} transmits the FFT magnitude bytes, sync bytes (\texttt{`F','F','T'}), and raw ADC samples via UART.

\vspace{-2mm}
\subsection{FFT Engine and Magnitude Computation}
The 2048-point pipelined radix-2 Cooley--Tukey FFT~\cite{ChenWang2020} runs at 200\,MHz, giving 48.828\,kHz resolution ($\Delta f = f_s/N = 100{\times}10^6/2048$) and 62.51\,$\mu$s latency, with ADC samples passing through the \texttt{decoder} before the FFT core. Spectral magnitude is:
\vspace{-1mm}
\begin{equation}
    |X[k]| = \sqrt{\mathrm{Re}[k]^2 + \mathrm{Im}[k]^2},
    \label{eq:magnitude}
\end{equation}
\noindent computed via \texttt{Square}, \texttt{Sum}, and CORDIC-based \texttt{Root} modules, yielding a 10-bit magnitude stored in \texttt{RAM\_FFT\_10bit}. The 48.828\,kHz/bin resolution is used for broadband spectral analysis to detect abnormal EM variations. For resolving low-frequency MT signals below 10\,kHz, decimation-based sub-band processing is planned as future work.
\vspace{-1mm}
\subsection{Key System Parameters}

The system runs on a Xilinx Artix-7 (XC7A100T, Nexys\,4\,DDR) FPGA with a single-channel 10-bit SAR differential ADC at 100\,MSPS (50\,MHz Nyquist). The 2048-point radix-2 pipelined FFT runs at 200\,MHz, giving 62.51\,$\mu$s latency and 48.828\,kHz resolution per bin. The current hardware acquires one MT field component at a time; for full five-component MT acquisition ($E_x$, $E_y$, $H_x$, $H_y$, $H_z$), five such channels can be instantiated by replicating the same RTL design with Clock Domain Crossing (CDC) handling, planned as future hardware revision. Post-implementation: 50 DSP slices (20.83\%), 7.5 Block RAMs (5.56\%), 0.333\,W power, with UART to the host PC.

The testbench bypasses UART timing by injecting the trigger byte directly via Verilog \texttt{force}/\texttt{release}, cutting latency from $\sim$8\,000 to 6 cycles. It is self-checking, passing only if the FSM returns to \texttt{wait\_state} after cycle 1000, confirming deadlock-free completion.

\subsection{FSM State Transition Verification}

The TOP module FSM was verified through all seven states in simulation: \texttt{wait\_state} (0\,ns, idle/polling UART) $\rightarrow$ \texttt{trig\_state} (1{,}075{,}000\,ns, on \texttt{`P'}) $\rightarrow$ \texttt{acq\_state} (1{,}305{,}000\,ns, ADC acquisition) $\rightarrow$ \texttt{fft\_state} (FFT activated) $\rightarrow$ \texttt{fft\_write} (bins written to RAM) $\rightarrow$ \texttt{square\_state} (magnitude computed) $\rightarrow$ \texttt{send\_state} (UART transmission).

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\vspace{-1mm}
\subsection{Conclusion}
\vspace{0mm}
This paper presented an FPGA-based MT DAQ and DSP system on Xilinx Artix-7 (Nexys\,4\,DDR), validated via behavioural simulation in Vivado\,2025, achieving 3067 LUTs (4.84\%), 50 DSPs (20.83\%), 7.5 BRAMs (5.56\%), and 0.333\,W estimated on-chip power. With a 100\,MSPS 10-bit ADC, 2048-point pipelined FFT, CORDIC magnitude computation, and seven-state FSM, it offers the highest sampling rate in the comparison group, suited for portable landslide monitoring in regions such as the Barak Valley.

\vspace{-1.5mm}
\subsection{Future Work}
\vspace{-0.5mm}
Future work includes \textbf{sensor integration and field trials}, \textbf{five-channel hardware expansion} with CDC for simultaneous $E_x$, $E_y$, $H_x$, $H_y$, $H_z$ acquisition, \textbf{decimation-based sub-band processing} for MT signals below 10\,kHz, \textbf{Zynq SoC migration} (ARM Cortex-A9 for GPS timestamping and network management), \textbf{on-chip anomaly detection} (lightweight FFT-based classifiers for pre-landslide EM signatures), and \textbf{hardware power measurement} as part of prototype validation.
